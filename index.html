Daybreak Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 9, 2026 · Last updated: November 12, 2026

1. What Daybreak does

Daybreak helps families check in on an older loved one who lives at home. It learns a normal daily routine from motion and door-sensor events reported by Ring devices, then sends family members a short daily summary and an alert if the routine changes. Daybreak is a check-in tool. It is not an emergency, medical, or monitoring-for-safety service.

This policy explains what information Daybreak collects, how it is used, who sees it, and the choices you have. In this policy, “the resident” means the older adult whose home devices are connected, and “family member” means a person the resident has invited to receive summaries and alerts.

2. Information we collect

From Ring, after the resident authorizes Daybreak:

  • Motion events from the connected Ring camera, including the type Ring reports (for example, person, animal, or vehicle) and the time of the event.
  • Open and close events from connected Ring contact sensors.
  • Device information and status, such as device names, device type, and whether a device is online or offline.
  • Account lifecycle events, such as when a device is added or removed or when the Ring subscription changes.

Daybreak does not collect or use live video, recorded video, video downloads, or audio. We do not call Ring’s livestream or video download features.

From the people who use Daybreak:

  • Name and contact details (email address and/or phone number) for the resident and for each invited family member.
  • Settings you choose, such as alert sensitivity, notification preferences, and sharing status.
  • Messages you send to our support team.

Information we create:

  • A learned routine for the household (for example, typical times of first activity and door use).
  • Daily summaries and alerts generated from that routine.

3. How we use information

We use the information above only to:

  • Learn a normal daily routine and detect when it changes.
  • Send daily summaries and alerts to family members the resident has approved.
  • Tell you when a device is offline, so a disconnected device is not mistaken for inactivity.
  • Operate, secure, troubleshoot, and improve Daybreak.
  • Respond to support requests and meet legal obligations.

We do not use your information for advertising. We do not sell your personal information, and we do not share it for third-party advertising.

AI and model training

We do not use Ring data to train AI or machine-learning models, and we do not allow our service providers to do so. AI services are used only to turn event data into plain-language summaries.

Because Ring data is never used for training, there is nothing to opt out of, and your service is not affected in any way. Pausing or stopping sharing, or disconnecting Daybreak, also does not change how any model is trained, because no training takes place.

When people may see your data

Daybreak does not collect video, images, or audio, so there is no footage for anyone to view. Event data (event types and times) is not reviewed by people as a routine matter. Daybreak personnel may access it only when needed to fix a problem you report, respond to a support request, keep the service secure, or meet a legal obligation.

4. Consent and the resident’s control

Daybreak is built so the resident stays in charge.

  • Daybreak connects to a resident’s Ring devices only after the Ring account holder authorizes it. We also ask whoever sets up Daybreak to confirm that the resident has agreed, or that they are the resident’s legally authorized representative.
  • The resident approves which family members receive information.
  • The resident can see what is shared with family, pause sharing at any time (for example, for 24 hours), and stop sharing entirely.
  • Family members only receive summaries, alerts, and door open/close times. They never receive video or audio from Daybreak.

If you are setting up Daybreak for someone else, please make sure they understand and agree before connecting their devices.

5. Who we share information with

We share information only in these limited cases:

  • Family members the resident has approved, limited to the summaries and alerts described above.
  • Service providers that help us run Daybreak, such as cloud hosting, notification delivery (email, text, or push), and AI services used to turn event data into plain-language summaries. For these AI services, we send only the type and time of events, not names or contact details, and they may not use that information to train their models. These providers may process information only on our behalf and under confidentiality and security obligations.
  • Ring and Amazon, because Daybreak runs through the Ring Appstore. Ring’s own privacy notice governs the information Ring collects and holds. This policy covers only what Daybreak receives and creates.
  • Legal and safety reasons, if we are required by law or valid legal process, or believe disclosure is necessary to protect someone’s safety or our rights.
  • A business transfer, if Daybreak is ever merged with or acquired by another company. We would tell you first and the same privacy commitments would continue to apply.

6. How long we keep information

  • Event data (motion, door, and device-status events): 90 days, then deleted or reduced to aggregate patterns that cannot identify a single event.
  • Learned routine and summaries: kept while the resident’s account is active, then deleted within 60 days of disconnection.
  • Contact details and settings: kept while your account is active.
  • Support messages: kept for 8 months.

When a resident disconnects Daybreak from Ring or deletes their account, we stop collecting new data, stop using the data we already hold, and delete it on the schedule above, except where the law requires us to keep it.

7. Your choices and rights

You can:

  • Pause or stop sharing at any time.
  • Disconnect Daybreak from your Ring account.
  • Ask for a copy of the information we hold about you.
  • Ask us to correct or delete your information.
  • Opt out of non-essential messages. Alerts you requested may continue until you turn them off.

To make a request, contact us at daybreak.support@protonmail.com. Depending on where you live (for example, California), you may have additional rights under local privacy law, and we will honor them. We will not discriminate against you for exercising your rights.

8. Security

We protect information with measures such as encryption in transit and at rest, access controls limited to people who need them, and signature verification of the event notifications we receive from Ring. No system is completely secure, so we cannot guarantee absolute security. If we learn of a breach that affects your information, we will notify you as required by law.

9. Not for emergencies

Daybreak can be delayed, can miss events, and can send alerts that turn out to be false alarms. It does not detect falls, medical problems, or emergencies. If you believe someone is in danger, call your local emergency number.

10. Children

Daybreak is intended for adults 18 and older. It is not directed to anyone under 18, and we do not knowingly collect their personal information. If you believe a child has given us information, contact us, and we will delete it.

11. Changes to this policy

If we change this policy in a meaningful way, we will update the date at the top and notify users by email or in the app before the change takes effect.

Changes to AI features

Daybreak does not run its own object-detection models. Motion and event detection come from Ring, and Daybreak uses AI only to write summaries. If we add or materially change an AI-powered feature, such as how summaries are generated or how routine changes are detected, we will tell you by email or in the app before it takes effect. We will describe what is changing and any known effect on accuracy. Alerts and summaries can always be delayed, incomplete, or inaccurate (see section 9).
